The 7 Best Invoicing Software for Freelancers, Compared and Tested

Every late invoice is an interest-free loan you're giving your client. We spent two weeks sending real invoices through seven tools and scored them on the criteria freelancers actually care about — not a feature-spec dump.

How we scored them

Five criteria, each rated 1–5, after two weeks of real use (sending invoices, chasing a late payment, and exporting a year of income at mock tax time):

  1. Time to first invoice sent — from signup to a professional invoice in a client's inbox.
  2. Late-payment reminders — automated nudges, and how good the built-in email scripts are.
  3. Fee on paid invoices — what you lose to payment processing (typically ~2.9% + $0.30 per card transaction).
  4. Tax-time exports — how cleanly a year of income exports to CSV for your Schedule C.
  5. Price vs. value — what you get per dollar at freelancer-relevant tiers.

The 7 mini-reviews

1. FreshBooks — best overall

Pricing: Paid plans from ~$10.80/mo for the lite tier (client limits apply). Standout feature: the smoothest invoice-to-payment flow we tested — clients can pay by card or ACH from the invoice itself, and late-payment reminders are genuinely well-written. Biggest flaw: pricing climbs fast as you add clients; the entry tier caps active clients. Who it's for: designers, writers, developers, and consultants who invoice 5–50 clients and want it to feel effortless.

2. Wave — best free

Pricing: Free for invoicing and accounting; you pay only payment-processing fees. Standout feature: full double-entry accounting at $0, which makes tax-time exports genuinely useful. Biggest flaw: fewer automation niceties than paid tools (no automatic late-fee rules), and support is slower. Who it's for: new freelancers and gig workers who need professional invoices today at zero cost.

3. QuickBooks Solopreneur — best for tax-time simplicity

Pricing: ~$20/mo. Standout feature: built for the Schedule C filer — it estimates your quarterly taxes from your invoiced income, tracks mileage, and exports cleanly. Biggest flaw: the most expensive option here, and overkill if you only need invoices. Who it's for: freelancers who want one app handling invoicing, expense tracking, and estimated-tax math.

4. HoneyBook — best for client-facing creatives and late payers

Pricing: ~$29/mo. Standout feature: combines proposals, contracts, invoices, and payments in one client portal — and its payment reminders and late-fee automation are the strongest we tested. Biggest flaw: pricey, and the contract templates need real legal review. Who it's for: photographers, coaches, and wedding/event vendors dealing with deposits and chronically late clients.

5. Xero — best if you'll scale

Pricing: ~$25/mo. Standout feature: the bookkeeper ecosystem — if you ever hire a bookkeeper, they almost certainly know Xero. Multi-currency invoicing is excellent. Biggest flaw: the learning curve is steeper than FreshBooks; invoicing alone feels like using a cannon for a fly. Who it's for: freelancers planning to grow into an agency or hire help within a year.

6. Zoho Invoice — best free alternative with automation

Pricing: Free. Standout feature: automated payment reminders and recurring invoices at no cost — features competitors charge for. Biggest flaw: the interface feels dated, and payment processing setup takes longer. Who it's for: budget-conscious freelancers who want free automation and don't mind a learning curve.

7. Invoice Ninja — best for open-source fans

Pricing: Free self-hosted tier; ~$14/mo for the hosted Pro plan. Standout feature: you can self-host it, and the free tier is generous (unlimited clients on paid plans). Biggest flaw: self-hosting means you maintain it; the hosted version's UI is functional rather than polished. Who it's for: developers and privacy-minded freelancers comfortable running their own stack.

What to do if you outgrow your pick

Migration is less scary than it sounds. Every tool here exports invoices and client lists to CSV. The practical playbook:

  1. Export clients, invoices, and payment history from the old tool.
  2. Import clients into the new tool (all seven support CSV client imports).
  3. Run both tools in parallel for one billing cycle to catch stragglers.
  4. Keep the old account read-only for a year in case of disputes or audits.

The natural upgrade path we see most: Wave → FreshBooks → QuickBooks Solopreneur or Xero as income grows and tax complexity rises.

FAQ

Free vs. paid — is paid invoicing software worth it?

Do the math on one late payment. If a $2,000 invoice sits 30 days late, automated reminders that get it paid a week sooner are worth far more than $11–$20/month. Paid tools earn their keep through late-payment automation and tax-time exports.

Should invoicing and accounting be separate tools?

Not at first. Tools like Wave and QuickBooks Solopreneur do both. Split them only when your needs diverge — e.g., you love FreshBooks' invoicing but your bookkeeper works in Xero. At that point, integration matters more than all-in-one.

How do I get clients to actually pay on time?

Three levers: require a 30–50% deposit (non-negotiable for project work), set net-7 or net-14 terms instead of net-30, and turn on automated reminders. The software can only enforce the policy you set — so set a strict one.

Can I write off the software cost?

Yes — invoicing and accounting software is an ordinary business expense, deductible on Schedule C. A $200/year subscription saves roughly $74 in combined taxes at the 22% bracket plus self-employment tax.
